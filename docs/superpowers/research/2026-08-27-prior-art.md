# Prior art survey — sim-racing coaching/telemetry tools

Date: 2026-08-27
Scope: informs the LMU AI coach roadmap. Read through the lens of our core
design principle — **corner identity (number/name/direction) is owned data
the tool computes/curates, never LLM-guessed.**

Confidence note: most of these products don't publish their internals.
Findings below are drawn from public marketing pages, docs, forums, and a
GitHub README, not from source code or hands-on testing. Where I couldn't
verify a claim, I've said so rather than guessing.

---

## CoachDave Academy (Delta)

**What it does:** Delta is a combined setup marketplace + telemetry/video
analysis tool for iRacing, ACC, and Le Mans Ultimate. Positioning is "buy a
pro setup, drive the same setup as the reference lap, compare your data to
theirs."

**Input data:** Standard driver-input/vehicle-dynamics channels — steering,
throttle, brake, gear, speed, lap time — pulled from the sim's own telemetry
feed.

**Corner identity handling (most relevant to us):** Delta ships **400+
pre-mapped tracks** with corner-by-corner breakdowns and sector splits, and
further divides each corner into a fixed 4-phase structure (braking, entry,
apex, exit) tagged as "SQ1, SQ2…" sequences. This is curated/pre-built data,
not something inferred per-lap from geometry — i.e., the same philosophy we
want: corner identity is a maintained asset, not a runtime guess.

**Coaching/analysis style:** "Auto Insights" AI layer compares your lap
phase-by-phase against a pro reference lap on the same setup, and states
deltas in driving terms ("began braking 6m later than reference") rather
than raw numbers. Video Analysis syncs onboard video frame-by-frame with the
telemetry overlay. Includes leaderboards for tracking improvement over time.

**Setup ("Setup IQ"):** 1000+ pro setups; some auto-tuning of setup based on
observed driving style; ACC-specific auto tire-pressure suggestions.

**Notable UX ideas:**
- Pairing a reference lap with the exact setup used to drive it (removes a
  confound — you're not comparing your car behavior to someone else's car).
- Fixed corner-phase vocabulary (brake/entry/apex/exit) as the atomic unit
  of coaching feedback, rather than freeform paragraph commentary.
- Leaderboard/progress tracking as a retention/motivation loop.

## Track Titan

**What it does:** AI coaching platform ("Strava for motorsport," per its
Dec 2025 $5M raise coverage) for iRacing, ACC, Assetto Corsa, F1 series,
Forza, and LMU. Auto-records sessions and analyzes telemetry to surface
where/why time is lost.

**Input data:** In-sim telemetry, auto-captured (no manual export step
described). Exact channel list not published.

**Corner identity handling:** Not documented publicly at the level of detail
we need. Marketing copy references "braking points, throttle application"
comparisons and per-corner mistakes, which implies some form of
corner/segment mapping, but Track Titan does not disclose whether that
mapping is hand-curated (like Delta's) or auto-detected from the driving
line. **Treat this as unverified — I could not confirm the mechanism.**

**Coaching/analysis style:** The headline feature is "Coaching Flows"
(shipped Nov 2025): instead of a corner-by-corner data dump, it identifies
the single biggest root cause of time loss on a lap and explains it in
driving language (e.g., distinguishing a late-throttle exit caused by your
own throttle hesitation vs. one forced by a bad entry). Tiered product:
free tier = unlimited recording + telemetry view, no AI tips; paid tiers add
AI tips, a personalized training plan, and pro setups. Claims ~0.5s
improvement after first analyzed session (unverified, self-reported).

**Notable UX ideas:**
- "Biggest root cause first" framing — prioritized, single most-impactful
  piece of feedback rather than an undifferentiated list of every deviation.
- Causal chain reasoning across corner phases (a mistake in entry is
  correctly attributed forward to its effect on exit), not just "you were
  slower here."
- Progressive monetization gate on *coaching* specifically, not on raw data
  access — free tier still gets full telemetry.

## AiM RaceStudio / MoTeC i2

**What it does:** Professional-grade (real-world motorsport, also used with
sim telemetry via loggers/converters) data analysis tools. Not a coaching
product — a data visualization/workbook tool for engineers and drivers to
build their own comparisons.

**Input data:** Raw channel logs (speed, GPS, throttle, brake pressure,
steering, suspension, etc.) from AiM/MoTeC loggers, or converted sim
telemetry (community tools exist to convert AiM RaceStudio logs to MoTeC i2
format and to convert sim telemetry — e.g. iRacing/rFactor2/AC — into MoTeC
log format).

**Corner identity handling (highly relevant to us):** This is the strongest
prior-art validation of our core principle. In i2, the user manually builds
a **track map** using a **beacon point** (start/finish reference) plus a
"corrected distance" calculation — GPS alone is not trusted for position.
On top of that map, the user manually defines **sections** ("Turn 1, Turn 2,
Straight 1-2…"), and the **Track Report** feature then computes min/max/avg
stats per section using those manually-defined boundaries. Corner identity
is 100% user-curated reference data, built once per track and reused across
every session — never inferred per-lap.

**Coaching/analysis style:** None built in — i2 is a workbook/worksheet
system (graphs, histograms, gauges, math channels) that the user assembles
themselves to do their own comparison and interpretation. No AI, no
prescriptive coaching. The "coaching" is entirely human expertise applied to
the visualization.

**Notable UX ideas:**
- Beacon + corrected-distance track-position model instead of raw GPS —
  relevant because sim data (which has ground-truth track-relative
  distance/lap-distance-percent from the sim engine itself) makes this even
  easier for us than it is for real-world telemetry.
- Track maps and section definitions are reusable per-track assets, built
  once and applied to every lap/session/driver afterward.
- Flexible workbook layout as a separate concern from the underlying
  section/corner model — the analysis views are just consumers of the
  curated corner data, not the source of it.

## Simulator-Controller

**What it does:** A large open-source (AutoHotkey-based) modular rig
controller/automation framework for sim racing (iRacing, ACC, and others),
built around a set of GPT-backed "AI crew" personas: **Jona** (race
engineer), **Cato** (race strategist), **Elisa** (spotter), and **Aiden**
(driving coach). Not a telemetry-analysis product first — a controller/
automation platform that happens to bolt AI personas onto it.

**Input data:** Live sim telemetry during a session, plus hardware
controller state (button box, Stream Deck, etc.).

**Corner identity handling:** Track identity here is infrastructural rather
than analytical: the project supports building a **track map** per
simulator/track combination, used for **"Track Automation"** — triggering
in-car setting changes (e.g., traction control level) automatically based
on where the car is on track. This is a coarser, automation-oriented
version of corner identity (trigger zones) rather than a coaching-oriented
one (named corner + phase breakdown), but it's built on the same idea: the
map is authored/configured once, not inferred live.

**Coaching/analysis style:** Aiden is a conversational GPT assistant (voice
or text) you can query mid-session or post-session; it reads telemetry,
compares it against a reference lap, and gives "corner by corner" verbal
instructions, plus a distinct "brake coaching" mode. Notably it supports
**local LLM runtimes** (Ollama/GPT4All, various local models) as an
alternative to cloud APIs — a privacy/cost-conscious architecture choice
worth noting given our own reliance on an LLM for the coaching-language
layer.

**Notable UX ideas:**
- Splitting the AI crew into separate personas by concern (engineer /
  strategist / spotter / coach) instead of one do-everything assistant.
- Voice-first, real-time in-session coaching delivery (vs. Delta/Track
  Titan's post-session review model).
- Local-LLM-as-fallback architecture, decoupling the coaching *language*
  generation from a specific cloud vendor.

## Ideas to triage

| Idea | Source | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curated per-track corner map (number/name/phase boundaries), built once and reused | Delta, MoTeC i2 | **v0.1** | Directly matches our core principle — this *is* the "corner identity is owned data" design, independently validated by two unrelated products. |
| Fixed corner-phase vocabulary (brake / entry / apex / exit) as the atomic feedback unit | Delta | **v0.1** | Cheap to encode once corner boundaries exist; gives the LLM a stable scaffold to talk about instead of raw traces. |
| "Biggest root cause first" prioritized feedback (not a dump of every deviation) | Track Titan | **v0.1** | Directly improves coaching UX and is a prompting/ranking concern, not new data infrastructure — low cost, high value. |
| Reference lap paired with the exact setup used to drive it | Delta | **v0.1** | Removes a real confound (car behavior vs. setup) for any delta comparison; we already need "current setup" as context. |
| Beacon-point + corrected-distance track positioning | MoTeC i2 | **reject** | Solves a real-world GPS-noise problem; LMU gives us exact sim-engine lap-distance already, so this problem doesn't exist for us. |
| Setup marketplace / pro setup downloads | Delta, Track Titan | **defer** | Valuable but orthogonal to the coaching core; needs licensing/community-sourcing infra we haven't scoped. Revisit post-v0.1. |
| Video analysis synced to telemetry | Delta | **defer** | High production value, but a separate capture/sync pipeline (video) with real cost; not needed to prove the coaching loop. |
| Leaderboards / progress tracking over time | Delta | **defer** | A retention feature for a multi-user product; we're not there yet — single-user tool first. |
| Multiple specialized AI personas (engineer/strategist/spotter/coach) | Simulator-Controller | **defer** | Good separation-of-concerns pattern, but premature — we have one job (setup + driving coach) and splitting it now adds orchestration cost with no current benefit. |
| Real-time voice coaching mid-session | Simulator-Controller | **reject (for v0.1)** | Our stated design is "coach between laps," not live-in-corner audio; different latency/UX budget and safety concerns (audio while driving). Could revisit later as a distinct feature. |
| Local-LLM fallback (Ollama/GPT4All) for the coaching-language layer | Simulator-Controller | **defer** | Legitimate architecture concern (cost/privacy), but adding a second LLM backend before we've validated the single cloud path is premature. |
| Free tier = full telemetry, paid tier = AI coaching | Track Titan | **reject** | A monetization/tiering decision, not a product-design idea; out of scope for this spike and premature before v0.1 exists. |
| Auto tire-pressure / auto-tuned setup suggestions from driving style | Delta | **defer** | Real coaching value ("setup coaching" is in our stated scope) but needs a working driving-style model first; sequence it after basic corner coaching works. |
